Research brief — Socratic and data-based verification inside a delegated research pipeline

Context:
- The maintainer runs a full-cycle delivery pipeline where Phase 3 research is delegated to
  Codex CLI (GPT-5.5, xhigh, read-only sandbox, live web tool). The research contract (a
  Codex skill) currently demands both-sides evidence: needed info, opposing views, evidence
  for, evidence against, unverified items, cited sources. One invocation per goal; the
  orchestrator (Claude Code) summarizes the artifact into GOAL.md.
- The maintainer now wants every research round to also: (1) verify its hypotheses and
  findings via Socratic questioning (elenchus: probe definitions, surface assumptions,
  demand evidence, seek counterexamples, test implications); (2) verify claims with DATA
  where feasible; (3) subject the data results themselves to the same Socratic
  examination. Design is open: same-invocation self-examination vs a second cross-examiner
  invocation vs orchestrator-side checks; what "data verification" can mean under a
  read-only sandbox (fetching primary datasets, reproducing calculations, quoting numbers)
  vs orchestrator-run executable checks.

Questions to research (prefer current primary sources; look for measured results):
1. Socratic questioning as an LLM verification protocol: published methods and results —
  Socratic prompting/questioning papers, SocREval or similar reference-free evaluation via
  the Socratic method, chain-of-verification (CoVe), self-ask, debate/critic frameworks,
  cross-examination setups (one model interrogating another's claims). What measurable
  gains in factuality/reasoning quality are reported, on what tasks, and what failure
  modes (performative or rubber-stamp dialogues, sycophantic agreement)?
2. Self-correction limits: evidence that LLM self-critique WITHOUT external feedback is
  unreliable (e.g., "LLMs cannot self-correct reasoning yet" line of work) versus evidence
  that critique with external grounding (tools, retrieved sources, executed code, fresh
  context) does help. Does a SEPARATE instance/context measurably outperform in-context
  self-questioning? Does role/persona separation matter?
3. Data-based verification in research workflows: prior art on verifying research claims
  by reproducing numbers from primary datasets or running small computations; risks
  documented for LLMs reading data (misreading statistics, cherry-picking, confusing
  correlation/causation, unit errors); checklists or protocols that catch data
  misinterpretation; how systems decide a claim is "checkable with data" vs not.
4. Pipeline design and cost: multi-pass research designs (gather → interrogate → verify)
  vs single-pass; reported cost/latency multipliers; diminishing returns; where the
  verification pass is best placed (inside the researcher, as a second adversarial pass,
  or at review time).
5. Both-sides on the goal itself:
  FOR — evidence that structured interrogation + data checks measurably reduce false or
  overconfident claims surviving into downstream decisions.
  AGAINST — the research contract is already adversarial (both-sides, cited, unverified
  section); marginal value of a Socratic layer may be low while cost doubles; a scripted
  Socratic dialogue authored by the same model may be theater rather than verification;
  simpler alternatives (mandatory executable checks only, or moving verification to the
  existing adversarial review phase) may capture most of the value.

Constraints:
- Codex research runs read-only with a live web tool; it cannot execute repository code.
  The orchestrator can run code locally. Web data is untrusted.
- The deliverable of the eventual change is markdown contract text (two skill files), not
  application code.
